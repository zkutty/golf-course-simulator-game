# Alpine Mountain content bible

Status: approved content direction for ZK-586 on 2026-07-31. It defines
playable high-country golf, not a ski resort, wilderness-survival game, or
real-location recreation. Final asset provenance and implementation
certification remain separate release gates.

## Identity

Alpine Mountain is a golf club set in a dramatic but believable high-country
valley. Conifers, exposed rock, meadow, cold clear water, and carefully used
elevation create memorable lines. A warm lodge anchors ordinary club life;
the mountain supports golf rather than overpowering it.

## Visual and spatial rules

- Keep major ridges and distant partial snow above or beyond the playable
  course. The routed golf land stays readable, reachable, and open enough for
  useful camera and shot decisions.
- Use granite, stream crossings, conifer edges, meadow, dormant turf, pale
  sand, wood, and stone. Terrain changes must clarify hazards and routes, not
  create impossible spectacle.
- The Vision preview needs a quiet dark-conifer/meadow caption-safe right
  third, one visible lodge, active golfers, maintenance, and a clearly
  playable fairway/green sequence.

## Seasons, care, and Design direction

Winter brings frost, healthy dormancy, and occasional high/natural-ground snow
without mandatory full closure. Freeze-thaw, short growing windows, exposed
weather, and expensive earthwork make cultivated surfaces costly; meadow and
conifer fit are comparatively resilient. The UI must distinguish snow/dormancy
from neglect or failed turf with pattern, icon, and text as well as color.

The Design dock should include playable turf, meadow, conifers, boulder/rock,
stream/water, pale bunker sand, rustic paths/bridges, lodge/service pieces,
and clear winter-risk information. Furniture is durable, compact, and practical
for a working golf property.

## Sound and narrative boundaries

Favor wind through conifers, moving water, occasional distant wildlife, club
activity, and weather. Never use ski-lift, avalanche, après-ski, or permanent
snowfield signals. Course closure, if weather requires it, is a golf-operation
decision rather than the biome's default identity.

## Prohibited shortcuts

- Ski resort, avalanche, summit-access spectacle, mandatory winter shutdown,
  or a course buried under snow.
- Vertical terrain that makes holes unreadable or breaks authoritative golf,
  service, or camera contracts.
- Protected resort/course likeness, logos, or an unexplained generated artifact.

## Approval checklist

- At overview and close zoom, the setting reads as mountain golf—distinct from
  snowy Parkland—with a believable playable route and active operation.
- Winter/dormancy, erosion/freeze-thaw pressure, and repair communication are
  understandable without color alone.
- Art, architecture, climate, gameplay, performance, provenance, and Vision
  crop review are recorded before the corresponding asset work is certified.

## Recorded decision

Project-owner approval: **a golf resort in the mountains**. Playable course
land and the working lodge remain primary; mountain spectacle supports golf.
